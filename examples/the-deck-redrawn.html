The deck,
redrawn

These three components already existed — hand-built, good-looking, and painted into canvas bitmaps. Canvas was the problem. A bitmap has no elements, so there was nothing to hang a mark on and nothing for a review to ask. The dials grew from zero whether or not a ratio had been measured. The globe turned at a constant rate over any feed, including a dead one — and a turning globe reads as health, which makes it the most expensive thing on a deck to get wrong.

MAGI consensus

A ratio drawn as an arc was always level — a measured quantity drawing itself to its measured extent. The third ring was never measured. It does not sweep to zero; it declines, and prints the reason.

On canvas all three grew identically from zero, because the growth was a loop rather than a reading. A ring at zero and a ring nobody filled in must not look alike.

The river — phosphor

One lane per subject on a real time axis. The beam is trace, staggered in the order the lanes are read. The trail fades by each lane's real age rather than by a constant — and refuses to fade at all when no clock was supplied.

The last lane has no run. It is drawn as an empty lane rather than left out, because an absent row and an empty row must not look alike.

The globe — live, then stale

The same component twice. The mesh stays canvas: a wireframe sphere is a few hundred arcs per frame, and as DOM it would be slower without being more honest — the mesh means "this is a globe", it carries no reading. So the seam moved. The endpoints are real elements over the bitmap, and the rotation is traffic: one turn per measured interval, or none.

Right-hand feed is stale, so it stops. One host is carrying nobody, so no arc is drawn to it — an arc between two points is the most convincing way a screen can claim flow.
